# Trellis reconstruction disposition

The reconstruction is visually coherent and operator-safe, but it does not
preserve the source image's three-legged topology. Across the six recorded
orbit views, Trellis resolves the subject as a conventional bilateral lizard:
two large hind legs, two small forelimbs, and a long tail. The source's third
major support has been absorbed into the tail/body prior rather than recovered
as an independently load-bearing leg.

This is useful negative evidence for the direct-image route. A plausible
single-view image of a three-legged creature is not, by itself, sufficient to
make this Trellis route reconstruct a tripod. The result does not rule out
stronger source plates, multiview conditioning, or an authored-envelope route.

Evidence:

- `output.glb`: reconstructed textured mesh.
- `orbit/`: six 700 x 700 inspection views at 60-degree azimuth intervals.
- `orbit-manifest.json`: render parameters and source identity.
- `metadata.json`: effective reconstruction route and input provenance.

Claim ceiling: topology-collapse evidence for this one source image and this
effective Trellis route only. It is not a general claim that Trellis cannot
reconstruct three-legged creatures.

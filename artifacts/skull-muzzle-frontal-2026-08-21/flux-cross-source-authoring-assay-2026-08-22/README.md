# Cross-Source Authoring Assay

This assay asks whether four FLUX edit transformations behave predictably across four materially different creature-head sources. The operator-preferred `soft-cel` and `feature-animation` columns are primary; `matte-resin` and `connected-planes` are structural controls.

All sixteen cells hold the effective route and generation seed fixed. The prompts share one source-neutral identity-preservation clause so the alternate sources are not asked to inherit the canonical horned creature's markings or anatomy. Camera pose remains source-authored and is therefore an explicit comparison limitation.

Open `index.html` for direct row-by-column inspection and in-place seed comparison. The visual interpretation is in `matrix-review.md`; requested and effective route identity, timings, hashes, output inventories, and failure phase are in `terminal-receipt.json` and `preferred-seed-81440-terminal-receipt.json`.

## Claim ceiling

The complete four-column matrix establishes directional cross-source repeatability at seed `81439`. The operator-preferred columns also have a second seed, `81440`, which may establish two-seed directional stability of macro behavior. Neither pass establishes universal prompt behavior or reconstruction quality.

## Result

The four transformations repeat directionally across all four sources, but they do not share one identity contract. Across two seeds, `Feature-animation sculptural` repeats a stable attached-shingle construction while preserving the source envelope; palette, eye completion, and finish remain seed-sensitive. `Soft cel-painted resin` repeats only as a broad polished character-authoring operation: exact markings, terminator, costume, and species fidelity remain seed-sensitive. `Beveled connected planes` is the most identity-conservative structural transform at the single tested seed. `Matte-resin gallery sculpture` preserves anatomy but may amplify latent markings and produced weak eyes on the canonical source.

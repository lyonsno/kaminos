# Multi-source sparse-guidance assay

This assay asks whether the sparse-structure CFG transition observed on source `0066` is a reusable creature-control law or a source-specific accident. It holds seed, steps, resolution, meshing, texture decode, cascade mode, sparse rescale/interval, and downstream Shape guidance fixed while applying sparse CFG `0.0`, `0.25`, and `0.5` to three materially different creature sources.

## Claim boundary

The first assay established that sparse-stage pressure can change gross topology while dense Shape pressure mainly changes local detail and surface congestion for one source. This assay tests source dependence and repeatability. The transition threshold remains provisional until all three source sections have exact effective route receipts and admitted multi-view witnesses.

The visual comparison may support a bounded control law: lower sparse guidance permits more model-prior invention, while higher sparse guidance preserves more source identity. It cannot establish that one fixed CFG value produces the same morphology regime for every source, seed, or prompt basin.

The visual read will score two axes independently. **Identity retention** means the source body axis, terminal orientation, broad contact layout, and dominant mass distribution remain recognizable. **Prior contribution** means Trellis introduces a materially different silhouette class, appendage vocabulary, segmentation rhythm, or whole-organism gestalt rather than merely roughening the supplied surface. A useful inspiration regime needs both; resemblance alone is tracing, while novelty without lineage is replacement.

## Evidence contract

- Nine Trellis generations: three sources by three sparse guidance strengths.
- One fixed seed and one exact effective route family.
- Four distinct Blender camera witnesses per generated GLB.
- Every contact-sheet cell binds its source job, camera route, PNG hash, and originating GLB hash.
- Missing, failed, duplicate, fallback, or route-divergent jobs fail artifact assembly.

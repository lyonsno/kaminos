# Current-K4 zero-slope occupancy transition assay

The normalized-sine-squared transition is a valid mechanism option and a visual non-solution.

It makes occupancy displacement approach fixed attachments with zero continuous slope rather than the reviewed normalized-sine profile's nonzero attachment slope. Requested and effective profile identity are explicit, invalid or out-of-mode use fails, and the same current-K4 construction identities, role schedule, taper, obstacle, and compartment remain bound.

At 64 iterations, the worst source-tangent cosine improves from `0.55198` to `0.58285` at baseline, `0.51740` to `0.55289` under mild crowding, and `0.48436` to `0.52664` under moderate crowding. Pairwise residual remains close to the reviewed baseline at `0.08397`, `0.08781`, and `0.09249`; endpoint drift and volume error remain zero. The trade is not uniformly favorable: skeletal residual increases to roughly `0.002` or less, and curvature-sign reversals change from `20/17/16` to `25/19/16`.

The pixels settle the decision. Across all three directly inspected result frames, the hard scheduled fan and visible purple lower-attachment hook remain. The clamped result is visually almost indistinguishable from the reviewed sine output and does not materially advance the accepted visual contract. It is rejected for continuous-clearance and anatomical admission.

The next causal lever is occupancy strength, not another scalar envelope. The same identity-bound role schedule should become a bounded soft target whose influence can be swept against compactness and separation. That assay must show source, reviewed full-strength result, and reduced-strength result together; a numeric Pareto row without a visibly less geometric cluster does not pass.

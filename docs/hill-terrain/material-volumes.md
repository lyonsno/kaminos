# Rock and soil material specimen

Open `?workspace=workbench&experiment=hill-volume` in the shared Workbench. The specimen starts paused. Choose the authored nine-solid outcrop or the hundred-rock fixture with soil between and around25four-piece source-supported columns. Initial soil height is adjustable and retained; changing it resets the fixture. The earlier heightfield formation remains a separate landscape reference.

**Play** evolves the material; **Advance 0.1 s** pauses and advances exact physical substeps. **Push material** applies a localized velocity impulse at the editable specimen-local center and radius. Each rock begins with a full pose anchor to the specimen frame. Choose a rock to release its anchor or weather its surface. A supported rock may remain still after release; **Nudge released rock** applies the editable velocity impulse to exercise its motion. Rocks can release independently or together through **Release all pose anchors**; free pieces collide with one another and with anchored rock. **Prescribed growth** moves the lower boundary and anchored rock with a smooth spatial drive; it is an explicit input, not a tectonic or ecological model. Native scene transforms position the whole specimen. Reset reconstructs its initial material state. Camera movement does not resample the material.

In Workbench, **Show pose anchors** reveals spatial pins with leaders to mounts on the rocks. Hover or focus a pin to highlight its rock’s source face and read what it holds: position and orientation relative to the specimen frame. Click the pin, or pull it away and release, to free that rock; Escape cancels a pull. Release targets the pin’s owner independently of the sidebar selection. The visibility choice is retained in `recipe()`.

The mount is an interaction/annotation location on the source surface. The current constraint holds the complete body pose; it is not a hinged attachment, a rock-to-rock bond patch, or a breakable spring. Anchor records derive from the same anchored state used by the solver. Their displayed positions follow prescribed growth analytically and native root transforms; they do not acquire per-frame GPU state. Weathering reconstructs the surface mount, and a generation check rejects a release aimed at an obsolete reconstruction.

Release a rock explicitly, then hover a visible rock face and drag in the intended shove direction. A dotted vector shows direction and distance-controlled strength; release applies a velocity impulse at that surface point, including angular response. The specimen pauses while aiming and restores prior playback after release or Escape cancellation. Authoring and empty-space camera gestures retain their existing behavior. “Drag released rocks to shove” disables the tool. “Shove strength” adjusts gain; the gain curve has diminishing sensitivity without a maximum impulse. Attached rocks receive a quiet attachment hint and are never released by a drag.

Picking executes a resident source-plane query against current GPU body poses. Pointer queries acquire a32-byte hit record; ordinary simulation still does not download material state. No predicted trajectory is drawn.

The operations behind these controls are available on `window.kaminosHillVolume` and through `window.kaminosWorkbenchExperiments.invoke`. `recipe()` retains initial configuration, editable settings and view. It does not retain an evolved simulation. `snapshot()` is an explicit diagnostic acquisition of complete material and rigid state; it is outside the playback path. `gpu()` exposes same-device state and geometry buffers, their generation, and the specimen-to-world transform for later consumers. No hand or fluid consumer is integrated by this experiment.

## Material construction and evolution

Persistent soil samples carry position, mass, reference volume, velocity, an APIC affine velocity gradient, pressure, deviatoric stress and accumulated compressive strain. A temporary three-dimensional grid gathers mass and momentum, applies stress and contact, and transfers velocity back to the samples. Quadratic 27-node APIC transfer follows the existing pinned Splash MLS machinery; the material and contact laws differ from the fluid solver.

The soil law is rate-form hypoelastic with a Jaumann stress update and a pressure-dependent deviatoric yield projection. Compression increases pressure; tension is released. The Frobenius norm of deviatoric stress is limited to the configured coefficient times pressure. This is a provisional granular continuum law, not a calibrated material or a finite-strain fracture model. Initial parameters are in SI units. The explicit timestep is checked against the elastic wave-speed condition.

Each rock retains a closed convex plane-defined boundary. Its volume, centroid and full inertia tensor come from exact closed-surface tetrahedron integrals. That integration is a preparation calculation; these tetrahedra are not a connected deformation mesh. Runtime rock state is a rigid pose and velocity. Soil contact contributes equal and opposite linear/angular impulses. Finite source triangles supply outside-corner distance; planes supply interior distance. Released fixture pieces use full convex separating-axis contact, including face normals and edge cross products, against free and anchored rock and the lower support. A single support-point impulse approximates the contact manifold. Long-settled pile fidelity and swept high-speed collision remain outside the demonstrated class.

Weathering is a bounded surface-recession operation: move the source planes inward, reconstruct the closed boundary, subtract its lost mass and add that mass as loose samples from the removed shell. It can therefore change near-vertical faces without a horizontal height sampling requirement. It is prescribed recession, not a moisture-driven weathering-rate model. Geometry reconstruction requires an explicit state acquisition; ordinary motion retains resident GPU state. Weathering must preserve the physical clock and current growth boundary.

## Starting poses and material ownership

In the small-outcrop reference, the retained authored drawing solids overlap by35mm at adjacent bed boundaries. Treating those overlapping volumes as separate released rigid bodies is an invalid initial mechanical state. The default specimen prepares nonpenetrating poses by upward constraint projection: the middle bed moves35mm and the upper bed70mm; every source face is retained. **Separate overlapping starting poses** resets and switches this preparation. Its unchecked state preserves the authored poses for construction inspection; release refuses that overlapping input. This reversible preparation changes the initial seams. It does not preserve the exact original union boundary, and does not establish a shared material partition for contiguous bedrock. That is a separate construction problem.

Human and agent actions share one queue, including settings changes, so a pending release, weathering or push retains its requested target/parameters. Reset before the first rendered frame defers reconstruction until the renderer is available. The witness rejects unknown phases instead of silently running a smaller verification route.

## Surface and limits

Rendering extracts a soil isosurface from the current GPU mass occupancy using six tetrahedra per grid cell. A GPU indirect draw uses the generated vertex count. The allocated maximum follows the mathematical maximum of twelve triangles per cell; it is not a contributor-retention cap. Rock rendering uses the same retained source geometry and current rigid pose as contact. Both update on the existing scene device without playback readback.

The initial surface is visibly faceted at the specimen's chosen grid spacing. It demonstrates material support and evolution, not naturalistic closeup detail or the macrostructure quality of the earlier formation system. General new cracks through intact rock, erosion by the live fluid solver, ecological succession, history-driven world processes and a permanent heightfield/volume coupling remain subsequent work. Prescribed growth exercises the mechanical response to a changing world boundary; it does not supply those causes.

Spatial contact continuation: the small outcrop and a hundred-rock pile are selectable initial fixtures in the same Workbench. The pile has 100 distinct, source-bounded nonoverlapping rock volumes and surrounding/interstitial soil; it is a controlled construction, not geological generation. Explicit snapshots and pointer acquisitions remain separate from playback; no material readback drives simulation. This implements the proposed scaling mechanism before benchmarking, as requested. Broad production performance, general high-speed swept collision, retained naturalistic soil relief and the reported unexpected second launch remain unestablished pending physical exercise.

Initial soil height is a retained fixture parameter, exposed as a drag slider that resets on release. The hundred-rock default peak is0.65m, exposing upper layers while retaining soil between and around the columns; deeper fill including1.28m remains available. Height is validated against the actual physical grid ceiling. The offline pile witness reads source planes and body poses from the actual GPU buffers, records all CPU-reference SAT contacts, rejects a collapsed-pose control and requires post-motion penetration below5mm. These are physical correctness checks, not timing measurements.

Interactive playback retains elapsed frame time as pending simulation credit. It first measures one physical substep, then uses the observed batch completion cost to choose the next batch against a16.7ms service budget. The budget controls how much work is submitted at once; it neither changes the physical timestep nor discards pending time. Slow service appears as time behind in the status. Pause/resume and explicit Advance establish a fresh interactive clock; Advance still executes its entire requested duration, submitting measured chunks and redrawing between them. This prevents catch-up batches from growing without feedback while keeping the difference between responsive controls and actual simulation throughput explicit.

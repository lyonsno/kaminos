# A Reactive Lagrangian Optical Integrator for Subgrid Flame Super-Resolution

This is the working title of the `optical-particles` Workbench experiment. The method combines a live Eulerian combustion/flow simulation with a continuously refreshed Lagrangian optical representation. Fresh local material observations seed carriers with source, intrinsic emission, extinction and represented support. Carriers retain their history and evolve between observations; the current view is integrated from that evolved medium.

The target is fine, coherent, moving flame and smoke at useful interactive cost. The useful regime preserves fine optical structure while keeping gross emission, extinction and visibility plausible. Dense refresh toward the instantaneous coarse-grid appearance is a comparison, rather than the sole quality objective. “Subgrid” and “super-resolution” describe the optical appearance relative to the Eulerian grid. They do not assert a validated higher-resolution velocity or chemical solution.

The active architecture is **Fine plus optional Coarse (World)**. Middle is superseded and outside this work's implementation and compatibility targets, per the operator's November 10 clarification. Historical fixture code still containing Middle wiring does not make it part of the architecture or a preservation requirement. This clarification calls for no Middle removal or repair on this branch. The current development phase uses the existing single-grid route. Coarse integration is intentionally deferred and will be revisited alongside the separate coupled-grid/performance work; retain its earlier result as a reference.

## Implemented state and integration

`volume-optical-particles.mjs` owns a persistent world-space pool with a96-byte record: position/age; captured scattered light and initial soot; intrinsic emission and initial fuel; residual velocity and representation-life clock; birth support/direction; and carried fuel, heat, soot and oxidizer. Births sample current Fine material and oxidizer, independently from the local optical observation. Clear fuel can be acquired before it emits. Composition is a trilinear sample of the stored grid channels; a separately reconstructed display material can differ. Current development rejects Coarse input explicitly. The native host requires the active Reactant law and its actual oxidizer field; an inactive law reports the carrier route unavailable and retains the ordinary volume display.

Carriers consume colocated fuel/oxidizer stoichiometrically, release heat, cool, create soot and retire soot using the shared simulation model and reference-time controls. The native oxygen/temperature-gated soot-burnout term is applied with the authored appearance calibration as well. Intrinsic emission then follows the native soot/temperature thermal LUT and fresh-burn spectrum. Captured scattered light retains birth illumination with independent gain and age fade. There is no current-field relighting or chemical reservoir gather on existing carriers.

`volume-particle-exchange.mjs` bins the live population with GPU count, exclusive prefix and scatter. Disjoint pairs within each bin partially mix carried composition, weighted by fixed birth pitch² × represented ray length. Pairing is linear in the live population and requires no nearest-neighbor search or all-pairs enumeration. The quadrature-weighted sum is conserved by each exchange; this is not a proof of physical domain-mass conservation under biased acquisition. Atomic scatter order within a cell is unspecified. Exchange strength, e-folding time and cell size are independently authored; zero strength preserves state. Large bins can mix distinct sheets and must be judged visually.

Representation lifetime is separate from chemical burn/cooling time. Fuel fraction, ignition-gated heat, soot activity and remaining quiet weight blend four positive authored lifetimes. A clock advances by strength × dt / blended lifetime, fades over an authored final fraction and retires at1. `stateLife=0` disables this extra clock. Maximum age remains an independent advanced cap; rotating-pool overwrite can replace carriers earlier. Presentation-only redraws freeze composition, clock and allocator cursor.

Emission and extinction use independent finite generalized-Gaussian profiles with optional smooth tails reaching zero at the existing2.5-radius quad edge. Profile normalization preserves the declared finite coefficient integral before exponential attenuation. It does not promise constant final alpha, constant3Dmass or identical antialiasing for every non-Gaussian shape. Deposition, self visibility and final attenuation share the extinction profile. Wider spatial radius can still increase overlap and optical amount.

The raster uses perspective-projected Gaussian footprints. Optional orientation follows the already sampled grid velocity. Depth modes use a uniform set of camera-depth slices over the retained depth range, with particle extinction deposition and a prefix queried during source accumulation. Each footprint currently deposits at its center depth; the method does not integrate a full 3D Gaussian through every intersected slice. The separate reference-march display remains a completed-image comparison, rather than a coherent grid/particle mixture.

## What reaction currently changes about motion

Every advancing carrier in this candidate samples the current Fine velocity. The **Grid flow transport** control scales that displacement. Independently, the implementation already carries a heat-driven residual velocity:

```text
H = updated carrier heat
d = exp(-dt / relaxationTime)
vResidualNext = d * vResidual + up * rise * H * relaxationTime * (1 - d)
positionNext = position + gridDisplacement + dt * vResidualNext
```

**Additional rise** (`rise`) drives this reaction-to-motion path. **Velocity relaxation** (`drag`) sets its relaxation time. `rise` defaults to zero; birth initializes residual velocity to zero. A previously nonzero residual decays after rise is set to zero. Enabling the existing control uses arithmetic already present in the update shader and adds no field queries. Changed trajectories and projected coverage can still affect total rendering cost.

**Reaction support expansion** (`expansion`) separately grows the footprint as initial fuel is consumed. It defaults to zero. It changes spatial support and optical amount, not the carrier center or the grid velocity. Flow alignment changes orientation, independently of this growth.

There is no feedback from carrier heat/soot into the Eulerian momentum or reaction solve, and no inter-carrier force model. Grid reaction continues to affect the grid velocity sampled by the carriers. With rise disabled and no remaining residual velocity, fine carrier-center motion follows that sampled field while fine reaction/optical state evolves independently.

Reaction age and residual motion use simulation-step delta times the effective reaction time scale. Grid displacement has its separate step/backtrace/flow factors. Rendered frame count is not the age unit. Inspect effective live settings before attributing a particular tune to rise or expansion; their zero defaults do not establish the values used in a later tune.

## Why the combined system can retain more detail

The state of the effect consists of the grid **and** the carrier population. The population retains earlier local observations, continuous trajectories and independently evolved reaction/optical state that cannot be reconstructed from the current grid snapshot alone. Some fine structure comes from retained observations and their transport; some is supplied by the continuation model and support choices. The system explicitly records that additional state in the carriers.

A fine material field transported by a coarser velocity field can remain a useful dynamical representation even without fine material feeding back into momentum. Stronger reactive motion is an available direction, but is not required for the existing representation to be richer than an instantaneous grid render. Its effect must be distinguished from buoyancy already supplied by the Eulerian simulation.

## Live controls and visibility comparison

The experiment groups display, acquisition/coverage, depth/visibility, population/renewal, support, reaction history and transport controls. Typing applies a complete valid value after a short debounce; scrubbing applies immediately. Invalid active text remains editable, leaves accepted settings intact and restores the accepted value when editing ends. Flow-following acquisition attempts a live-carrier proposal at the authored fraction, accepts according to normalized fuel/heat/soot priorities, predicts current advection/residual motion and adds authored Fine-cell jitter. Proposals outside the domain/frustum are declined; scene depth can reject hidden proposals. The native source ray evaluates the current material at that actual position/depth. When tracking does not produce a proposal, acquisition uses the existing analytic emitter/Fine/broad sampler. Reported Source/Fine/Broad shares are conditional on that fallback, not measured total birth percentages. The population readout reports consumed follow rows separately. Source history still estimates inlet travel for fallback; it is not a substitute for the flow-following path.

**Read population now** obtains a compact GPU snapshot of active carriers, anchored evaluation rows, optically nonzero rows, intrinsic-emission rows and consumed flow-following rows, extinctive carriers and ages. Rotating-pool accepted/placed births come from its actual cursor counter. **Live renewal readouts** additionally tracks overwritten, expired and outside-grid carriers and samples the readout once per second. Untracked fields remain unavailable. The capacity/placed-birth estimate is an overwrite horizon at that sampled update's rate, not a guaranteed lifetime. Simulation-step delta and scaled reaction-time delta are distinct. Diagnostics add work and should be disabled for cost comparisons.

In a single-grid configuration, advancing carriers retire before or after transport if they are outside the admitted grid. Zero velocity inside the grid remains valid. This removes stale optical footprints without reducing the capacity-wide dispatch or allocated storage. Existing coupled-grid wiring remains outside this phase's active implementation.

**Fill missing extinction** supplies current Fine-grid extinction where its cumulative optical depth exceeds the particle prefix. Strength0 with normal view is the original depth renderer. **Extinction slack** permits a cumulative difference before the assist intervenes. **Extinction view** offers Normal, Missing extinction and Suppressed light; diagnostic views preview full-strength assistance while leaving the population untouched.

The independent field traverses the prepared single-grid extinction coefficients as a piecewise-constant cell field, recording a prefix in the same camera-depth partition. It includes extinction before the first retained particle and after the last for background transmission, with ordinary scene-depth clipping. It evaluates no emission or illumination. The prepared cell coefficients are an independent coarse reference, not an exact copy of every camera-material detail. Coarse integration and non-perspective assist are outside this implementation; stale/missing coefficient fields are refused visibly. With assist/views off, the independent field is absent.

For each matching cumulative interval, `tauEffective = tauParticle + strength * max(0, tauGrid - tauParticle - slack)`. Source transfer uses effective prefix differences with the selected within-slice convention, while final background transmission includes the full current-grid deficit. Particle state, depth partition, support and renewal remain unchanged by assist/view edits. Missing-extinction view shows the final optical-depth deficit; Suppressed light shows removed source contribution. The assist adds absorption rather than newly evaluated scattered illumination. It can reveal consequential missing opacity even when excessive intrinsic continuation also exists.

## Recovered next-work plan

The live controls and single-grid extinction comparison above are implemented. The following keeps the broader continuation plan, including remaining research contenders; the manually tunable particle medium remains the working baseline.

1. **Diagnose long-lived glow after the single-grid retirement repair.** Active single-grid retirement is implemented; a later Coarse return can address domain transfers. Follow identified carriers through potential, heat, soot, intrinsic emission, captured scattering, age and motion to distinguish slow evolution from replacement or stale light. Ordinary acquisition can continue while identities are traced. Inactive records can stop rasterizing, but the current capacity-wide dispatch/draw and rotating allocator do not immediately turn every retired record into compute savings or prioritize its hole for new births.
2. **Develop the exposed renewal clocks and ratios together.** Distinguish fresh evaluations, valid observations, accepted/placed births, allocated capacity, active population and overwrite horizon. Group acquisition/placement, birth/turnover, age/state clocks and support/overlap controls. The compact snapshot and optional live diagnostics distinguish active/birth counts from budgets. Keep simulation-reference units explicit.
3. **Track coverage from real observations.** Improve the analytic source corridor using admitted fresh observations, with exploration outside it for bends and new tongues. Source history currently estimates inlet travel; it is not recorded turbulent history. Use separate pre-attenuation emissive and extinctive coverage, qualified by depth where necessary. A visually filled image or the retained particles alone cannot certify missing material as absent.
4. **Use the live foreground-visibility control.** “Fill missing extinction” strength compares an independently built current Fine/Coarse extinction prefix with the particle prefix over matching camera-depth intervals. Start from `tauEffective = tauParticle + strength * max(0, tauGrid - tauParticle - slack)`. Strength 0 preserves the present renderer; strength 1 with zero slack raises cumulative optical depth to the greater of the two. Apply the resulting prefix consistently to source attenuation, within-slice transfer and final background transmission, rather than adding the complete grid opacity over already represented particle opacity. Preserve carrier state, birth policy, support and particle depth partition when the strength changes. The views show the optical-depth deficit or the particle light the assist suppresses. This lets the operator explore the hypothesis directly without a separate assay. Independent current extinction can expose missing visibility while a carrier reaction error still coexists; a favorable image alone does not establish that reaction is correct. The added pass queries extinction, with no emission/scattering-light evaluation; its cost remains configuration-dependent and unmeasured here. It adds absorption rather than newly evaluated scattered illumination. Coarse scale/slack remain controls for preserving useful fine history.
5. **Make multiple local evaluations and larger support testable.** One fresh material evaluation per ray is hardwired in the prototype, not a measured optimum. Separate evaluation budget, depth samples per ray, neighboring-ray extent, represented ray depth, transverse support and retained capacity. Reuse existing observations where possible. Extra depth samples alone cannot measure a wider transverse footprint. Fit means/spread/residual before choosing covariance changes, positive splits or a different kernel.
6. **Use source-frame structural stability appropriately.** A stable tongue can contain rapidly moving and reacting material. Stability can justify reusing support geometry/basis while optical coefficients or material observations change quickly. Preserve discovery near emitters and burning objects. Do not infer a longer parcel lifetime solely from a slowly changing source-relative shape. Larger support must earn savings after its added overlap is charged; depth extent spanning bins requires a corresponding deposition model beyond the current center slice.
7. **Relight material when that improves the result.** Independent captured-light fading is already implemented. Current-field relighting would retain the required scattering coefficients and sample the prepared same-generation incident field. That is distinct from solving new light transport for every particle. Its record layout, queries and optical semantics remain to be evaluated. A moving light over upper smoke is a useful diagnostic because near-flame illumination can hide stale-light behavior.
8. **Keep representation mixtures available.** A broad smoke march could contribute support and optical terms to the same depth integration while particles supply fine structure, additional material or continuation. Explicit overlap weights/residual roles avoid accidental double counting. This is not an exclusive flame/smoke handoff and is not selected as a replacement for the working all-particle regime. Manual slice count and XY scale remain the first visibility controls; adaptive depth, moment visibility, learned/shared support context remain later contenders.

For stronger reaction-driven motion, begin by distinguishing the existing heat-driven rise and consumed-potential support growth from base grid transport. A residual buoyancy/expansion model could then be compared with that baseline. Reapplying the grid's bulk buoyancy blindly could distort a good flame. Cell-local composition exchange is implemented. New velocity solves, physical contact models and grid feedback remain separate architectural choices.

### Connected carried-material exchange and work controls

The single-Fine-grid carrier experiment now uses an overlapping composition field in `volume-particle-field.mjs`. Count/prefix/scatter retains every in-domain live carrier ID. Each field node reduces the weighted F/H/S/O state from its eight touching bins; each carrier gathers its eight surrounding nodes. Matching trilinear weights connect exchange across bin faces and preserve the declared weighted sums for constant per-component exchange fractions. The weight is the existing birth-pitch squared times represented ray length. It is quadrature weight, not physical gas mass. Records remain 96 bytes. This changes composition exchange; it adds no particle pressure solve.

`exchangeHeat`, `exchangeSpecies`, and `exchangeSoot` scale the independent exchange responses; `exchangeRate` scales all three. Rates above one are supported. `exchangeEvery` schedules field passes by simulation steps and accumulates elapsed reaction time. `exchangeSpacing` authors node spacing in Fine-normalized domain units (Fine width 2); zero uses `exchangeCellSize` in Fine cells. The field and grouping have separate hard gates. Tracking requires grouping even when exchange is disabled. With tracking at zero and exchange disabled, count/prefix/scatter is skipped.

Acquisition and birth acceptance are separate gates. `acquisitionEnabled` / `acquisitionEvery` skip fresh source rendering and follow proposals without replacing the particle renderer. A skipped acquisition has zero effective observations and cannot birth from the previous batch. `birthsEnabled` suppresses births while keeping acquisition available. `updateEnabled` holds retained reaction, transport, age and survival; `reactionEnabled` and `transportEnabled` independently remove those parts of an advancing update. Exchange remains independently controllable. These controls preserve the pool. Direct refresh of an existing carrier’s captured illumination is a separate, still-unimplemented sampling operation.

The footprint selector offers ordinary Gaussian evaluation with smooth edge taper or the existing generalized falloff. Saved non-Gaussian profiles select the generalized route when the selector is absent. Both paths preserve their profile normalization. `maxFootprint` is an authored drawn-diameter ceiling in display pixels; zero disables it. `largeSupportFade` weakens oversized supports, and `opticalCutoff` skips weak billboards before projection. Clipping or weakening changes represented optical amount. Depth deposition may filter supports onto its coarser pixel grid.

Viewport telemetry reports configured versus actual fresh budgets, update/draw slots, allocation sizes, field dimensions and the step of the compact grouping count. The in-domain count is taken before advance/births; it is explicitly approximate and can age while grouping is off. Passive timestamps sample ordinary presented frames, never request an extra frame, and name the sampled frame/step/route. They are particle GPU pass intervals, including waits; they exclude shared simulation, lighting and preparation and cannot be summed as exclusive whole-frame costs.

The material menu distinguishes **Grouping bins** (the count/scatter indexing) from **Exchange-neighborhood heat** (a trilinear gather from the actual last reduced field buffer). Field heat reports its sampled simulation step and remains explicitly unavailable before a field build. A held field can differ from current carried heat after reaction, transport or a spacing edit; the view retains the field’s own spacing and dimensions.

Paused draws skip population update and birth passes. A paused acquisition-policy edit does not evaluate or mark a batch until simulation advances; explicit pool reset/structural bootstrap can still seed a new pool while paused. Birth-only updates remain possible with carrier advance disabled and reset their own renewal counters each advancing draw. Exchange telemetry separates `planned` allocation from `residentGrouping` and the last reduced `field`; the HUD keeps the field’s sample step and shows a different next plan explicitly. Visibility changes and explicit control edits bypass the HUD’s ordinary frame throttle.

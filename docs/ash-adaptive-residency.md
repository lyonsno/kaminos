# Automatic fine ash around scene colliders

New native ash fields use a coarse covering grid (twice the requested cell width), a reusable pool of 8³ fine-cell bricks, and an additional reusable pool at half that fine pitch. The extra level follows current collider detail and is prioritized by its projected size in the current camera. Older two-level fields retain their saved route. **Extra detail near the camera** enables the third level through the normal clearing reset. The field, source objects, transforms, scene saving and interaction stay in the ordinary scene editor. Existing uniform and explicitly refined saved fields keep their original routes. In **Adaptive detail**, choosing Automatic and resetting converts an older field; reset clears its deposited and falling material, as with a cell-width change.

**Fine capacity (% of field)** controls reserved storage, initially 12.5%. It is an exposed experimental memory allowance, not a promise that every requested location receives fine cells. **Show fine bricks** draws the resident addresses directly from GPU metadata. **Inspect fine residency** takes an explicit diagnostic snapshot and reports capacity, retained history and unmet requests. No automatic residency planning or live evolution maps a GPU buffer to the CPU. Saving and requested diagnostics do read state.

## Demand and maintenance

The existing collider producer caches object-local occupancy and updates only affected field tiles when geometry or poses change. Automatic fields build that cache at the requested fine pitch where the producer's existing capacity permits. During affected coarse-tile stamping, eight fine occupancy samples per coarse cell compare the four vertical fine columns. Horizontal disagreement, or fine support missed by the coarse center, requests direct detail. Uniform horizontal support already seen by the center creates no direct request. Coarse occupancy covers the center and all eight fine samples, so a full fine pool cannot discard already sampled thin supports. This is a local occupancy-representation signal, not a measured mesh-distance bound or recovery of detail already removed by a collider LOD.

The request map lives on the GPU. Direct feature demand takes priority over one brick of surrounding headroom. Empty headroom can yield just enough slots for missing direct demand; occupied history cannot be displaced by this priority. The allocator reuses empty slots, installs both directions of the owner map, and transfers coarse volume into the new fine columns conservatively. It does not require a full finest-resolution backing field; the explicit capacity setting can reserve one when requested. Falling deposition, contact, conservative exchange, collision and drawing use the same runtime ownership map. Collision clears/stamps a compact GPU list of dirty fine bricks using the existing cached shapes.

Occupied fine bricks remain at their deposited world-field locations after a collider moves. Empty bricks with no remaining demand can be reused. When the pool fills, new unmet locations remain coarse; retained mass is never dropped to make space. A long evolving landscape can therefore exhaust the initial allowance. Raising it currently requires the normal reset; automatic pool growth, lossy demotion and streamed history are not implemented. This is the explicit first residency policy rather than a claim that bounded storage admits unlimited fine history.

Planning runs after collider changes, and retries blocked demand when fine-cell evacuation may have freed storage. A full unchanged pool does not repeatedly scan all requests. Geometry/pose collection remains the existing CPU scene metadata path; demand, slot assignment, ownership changes and material transfer are GPU resident. The changed-tile metric costs up to eight cached shape queries per coarse cell, once per affected pose/geometry update; it is not free. The GPU still visits compact metadata and submits passes while idle. No total-cost or 1 ms claim follows from this architecture.

## Persistence and verification

`kaminos.adaptive-ash.v1` stores the authoritative owner/inverse maps with deposited density, pending tile work, the volume ledger and falling parcels. Restore rejects inconsistent maps, duplicated owners, unowned mass, invalid volume, and incompatible scene geometry/poses before uploading density. Demand and collision are derived again from the restored scene; the retained fine map is preserved. All rejectable checkpoint fields, including sliding, are validated before topology or material uploads. Geometry and topology changes do not reinitialize the field.

Small deterministic checks:

```sh
npm test -- --only tests/ash-adaptive-residency.mjs,tests/ash-adaptive-gpu.mjs,tests/ash-adaptive-collision-gpu.mjs,tests/ash-refined-surface-gpu.mjs,tests/ash-refinement.mjs,tests/ash-scene-object.mjs,tests/ash-scene-recovery.mjs
```

The GPU test uses the versioned Shader Herder dependency and records hardware versus compiler-only authority. It exercises the production allocation and exchange kernels, conservation through prolongation, history pinning, capacity pressure, reuse, settled maintenance and mixed-owner CPU/GPU numerical agreement. `tools/ash-adaptive-native-witness.mjs` exercises actual native moving geometry, GPU-produced fine residency, retained contact history, source deposition, rendering and full scene reopen. It records source hashes, effective source root, browser route, primary failure phase and browser cleanup. Reports and raw checkpoints are caller-owned output paths. A wrong-source route fails before opening a browser.

Coarse and fine faces retain their physical cell fill heights. Smooth lighting changes normals rather than bending vertices across stacked material layers; the geometry deliberately remains faceted.

The derived surface cache can use two words per padded tile cell: a visible-face mask and its cumulative face count. Dirty tiles rebuild masks and their GPU prefixes; the vertex shader decodes an instance rank back to the same physical face. Automatic selection uses this encoding only when its reservation is smaller than the original face-record cache. `runtime.surfaceCache` reports the effective encoding and byte reservation. The original encoding remains selectable through the computational runtime's `surfaceEncoding` option for comparison. Material, ownership and checkpoint layout stay identical across encodings; restore rebuilds the derived cache. This storage experiment adds prefix construction and vertex decoding work, so lower allocation alone does not establish lower frame cost.

The existing collision texture bound and legacy LOD precision compromises remain. Fine regions currently follow collider detail and its headroom, rather than independently detecting every evolving ash-surface feature. Dense moving kiln coverage, visual character and whole-consumer cost still need evidence at their actual scene scale. Performance comparisons belong in an uncontended Greenroom window; functional captures do not price the effect.

## Extra view-prioritized physical detail

With `refinement.levels: 3`, the requested `cellSize` still names the existing fine pitch. The covering grid keeps its old width; the new receiving cells are half the requested width. `layout.cellSize` reports that actual finest pitch, with logical cell widths 4, 2 and 1. The second pool initially reserves the same number of bricks as the first (computational callers can set `detailCapacity`). It is not a full finest-resolution backing field. Airborne parcel count and physical parcel size remain independent of that extra contact resolution.

Dirty middle-brick collision stamps compare eight finest cached occupancy samples, using the same feature-disagreement test as the first level. Their request map stays on the GPU. The current field-to-clip matrix and viewport arrive as ordinary CPU scene/camera metadata. Requests smaller than three projected pixels at the middle-cell pitch remain middle resolution; larger footprints enter ordered priority buckets. A newly prominent feature can replace empty lower-priority detail. Deposited finest history remains pinned even off screen or after a collider moves. Camera changes do not scan material: cached dirty-surface reduction maintains each tile's occupied-cell count, and planning reads that compact summary. Changed geometry or projection triggers planning; an unchanged scene and view sleep it.

A finest brick is admitted only when surrounding middle owners provide 2:1 balance. Those supporting middle bricks remain pinned while needed. Capacity pressure and missing balancing support leave the requested region at its existing resolution, with separate unmet and balance-blocked counts in **Inspect fine residency**. The same owner map governs conservative exchange, falling deposition, collider stamping and physical surface drawing. Prolongation preserves volume and column fill height; the physical state drives the added detail.

Three-level checkpoints use `kaminos.adaptive-ash.v2`, preserving both owner/inverse pairs, density and the existing continuation. Restore validates parent balance and reconstructs occupancy summaries before allocation can reuse empty storage. Old `v1` records retain their two-level interpretation. Enabling the extra level on an existing field uses the disclosed reset and clears ash; no history migration is implied.

The collision cache's existing 128-axis limit and chosen collider LOD still bound recoverable features. Projection priority cannot recover geometry absent from that cache. The extra physical level adds work and storage; no speedup or total frame-budget claim follows from functional evidence. Performance pricing is deferred until the requested implementation is assembled.

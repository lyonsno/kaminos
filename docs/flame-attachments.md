# Attach flame to an authored object

Choose **Flame attachments · authored Compound Crown** in the Workbench experiment chooser. It adds an ordinary authored apparatus, using Hostile's mechanism evaluator and geometry. Select it to edit Iris opening and Vane drive in Properties. Native scene transforms, duplication, deletion and scene saving remain the editing path; closing the experiment leaves the authored apparatus in the scene.

Select a supported mesh and use **Flame attachment → Attach flame**. The same attachment works on a GLB, procedural mesh or source-owned PBR object. Choose a manual radius or an exposed aperture output. Compound Crown exposes its clear inscribed radius. This is explicitly a round-source approximation, not the full complement of its iris leaf polygons. Vane drive moves the register; it supplies no physical swirl law.

Set the local outlet X/Y/Z and aim, then adjust radius (or the output multiplier), exit speed, fuel and inlet heat. Coordinates and manual radius use the object's original local scene units, including its authored root scale. Hostile's current iris leaves lie near local Y1.37 and the ceramic mouth near Y1.67. The intended functional outlet plane is a producer/consumer question; the control exposes the chosen plane instead of silently choosing one. Feed, fuel and heat are normalized existing solver controls, not calibrated hardware units. Aperture edits hold exit speed; total nominal supply changes with area.

The existing Fire & smoke field receives attachments through its immersed-source route. The attachment does not allocate a field or move evolved gas. The status shows source-centre admission, requested diameter in effective grid cells, and when the solver's minimum footprint enlarges the source. Source-centre admission alone does not prove complete aperture support, collision clearance, or resolved jet geometry. Moving outside the field disables supply under the existing source law. Hiding the object hides its contribution; existing gas can remain. Removing the attachment stops its supply while retaining the object.

Ordinary Duplicate creates another independent apparatus and attachment. Linked geometry repetitions currently share the one source attached to their authored root; their instance count and this limitation appear in the attachment status. Repeating source inputs along with linked geometry is a subsequent adapter slice.

The retained description is `flameAttachment`, schema `kaminos.flame-attachment.v1`. A manual radius is `{value: 0.2}`; an evaluated binding is `{output: "clearRadius", factor: 1}`. The compiler knows radius/frame/supply semantics and has no burner design-ID switch. Source owners expose named outputs; unavailable selected outputs fail explicitly. Compound Crown's `compoundCrown` payload belongs to its record domain and reconstructs through the common record service. Scene version12 prevents older clients from silently discarding attachment behavior.

The native operations are `window.kaminosFlameAttachments.set(id, attachmentOrNull)`, `.read(id)`, `.outputs(id)`, `.setMechanism(id, patch)` and `.addCrown()`. Inspector controls use the existing edit ledger for gestures, cancellation and Undo/Redo. Authored reconstruction is separate from retaining evolved gas. The current source representation and fixed-world-field limits remain visible design constraints.

# Spatial Asset Kiln

Kaminos is a spatial asset kiln: a browser-native place where generated assets,
route outputs, world fragments, and cartridges become staged, inspected,
conditioned, witnessed, promoted, and played with while keeping lineage close to
the visible object.

The public frame is simple: generated work gains a body in a spatial forge.

The goal is a spatial workbench where generated artifacts, route outputs, scene
states, and world evidence keep their provenance while gaining a body the
operator can inspect. A splat, material bake, terrain packet, motion preview,
world-state receipt, or cartridge scene arrives with its source route and
authority visible. Kaminos hosts the visible process, captures witnesses, and
keeps source evidence attached to the body on screen.

## Kiln Language

Creative inference has a brittle default rhythm: commit an experiment, wait
while the machine works, then inspect an output after curiosity has cooled.
Kaminos turns that wait into visible process.

The kiln language gives route work material phases:

- **Preheat:** inputs, source refs, backends, and effective route parameters are
  being prepared.
- **Burn:** live compute is spending and the work is materially in flight.
- **Bank:** outputs are settling, linking, indexing, or waiting for inspection.
- **Cool:** artifacts are present, witnessed, and stable enough to handle.
- **Glow:** cached or recalled work remains warm enough to understand its prior
  route and authority.
- **Snuff:** failure has a visible collapse with failure phase and last
  trustworthy evidence.

These phases are interface contracts and animation ideas at once. Live compute
earns full burn. Cached work earns residual warmth. Fixture and fallback routes
carry weaker heat. Stale or visual-only evidence carries a downgraded material
state. Failure has a visible collapse.

The realtime WebGPU fire and smoke volume is the first substrate for this
language. It gives the kiln a material vocabulary for heat, pressure, smoke,
banked coals, collapse, and route-local debug state.

## Spatial Pre-Production Forge

Kaminos is a spatial pre-production forge for game-like and world-like projects.
Agents and technical makers can open a Kaminos worktree, compose shared
affordances into a route, smoke it in the browser scene, and then graduate the
proven pieces by custody.

The core workflow is:

1. Start from a world chamber, workbench, asset route, or world cartridge.
2. Bring source-owned law, artifacts, endpoints, or state streams into the
   Kaminos worktree as dependencies.
3. Compose with Kaminos affordances: camera, fire, actors, stations, direct
   asset routes, Preview Benches, capture, witnesses, and Smoke Offers.
4. Run the same route the operator will smoke.
5. Capture screenshot or filmstrip evidence with route identity and source
   lineage.
6. Record graduation accounting for the pieces that should remain in Kaminos,
   move into a game repo, become shared runtime, ship as a Kaminos-backed shell,
   or archive as prototype evidence.

This workflow lets Kaminos serve both agent-assisted maker work and future
taste-first world creation.

## World Cartridges

A **World Cartridge** is a portable world seed for Kaminos. It gathers a world
identity, creature or actor defaults, scene recipes, terrains, generation
basins, behavior presets, source bridges, affordance bindings, witness routes,
and graduation notes into one openable package.

A cartridge can present a terrarium: a bounded world surface where a user can
observe, grow, farm, mutate, select, play, and capture world inhabitants. The
same cartridge can also serve as an agent composition target inside a Kaminos
worktree.

The first worked direction is a LERMS terrarium cartridge. It can combine little
bodies, Hill terrain, hand surfaces, creature behavior, generated assets, route
fire, and operator smoke capture. The LERMS game can develop its runtime,
renderer, loop, save model, and domain-native systems while the Kaminos
cartridge gives the forge a living world pack and worked example.

See [World Cartridges](world-cartridges.md) for the cartridge shape and
graduation modes.

## World Chambers

A **World Chamber** is a coherent place for a source-owned world, route, world
cartridge, or evidence space. It is the frame that lets an operator enter a
world-state surface with identity and spatial continuity.

Current and near-term chambers include Underhill-style staging areas: terrain,
actor, motion, object, and interaction evidence can be inspected together as a
world-in-progress. A chamber can carry a route receipt, source refs, camera
presets, source authority, freshness, fallback state, and witness artifacts.

When a chamber hosts terrain evidence from one source lane and motion evidence
from another, Kaminos preserves those source schemas and authorities beside the
hosted scene. The chamber becomes a shared place where the pieces can meet.

## Workbench/Kiln Surfaces

A **Workbench/Kiln** surface is where generated pieces and world fragments become
inspectable. It is the operational layer between raw source payloads and
inhabited scene/world presence.

Workbench/Kiln surfaces can stage assets, condition generated pieces, expose route
state, show witness receipts, and prepare promotion into a chamber, cartridge,
or scene. They make the operator's normal actions cheap: open the evidence,
change camera, inspect provenance, capture a screenshot or filmstrip, and route
a note back to the producer with the observed evidence attached.

This is where the kiln metaphor becomes operational. A bench can show whether a
generated piece is live, cached, fixture, fallback, stale, failed, or promoted.
Those states stay visible beside the visible object.

## Promotion And Graduation

Promotion moves a piece of work closer to the scene. The promotion ladder is:
scratch output -> witnessed payload -> promoted take/asset/sidecar -> chamber,
cartridge, or scene placement. Each step says what changed: stronger source,
stronger witness, operator acceptance, route correction, sidecar update, or
placement into a world chamber.

Graduation chooses the destination for a proven composition:

- **Remain in Kaminos terrarium:** the result becomes a cartridge feature,
  example scene, maker affordance, or taste-first interaction.
- **Port domain-native:** the source project implements the discovered behavior,
  state stream, interaction, or body law in its own product repo.
- **Extract shared runtime:** repeated needs across Kaminos and project repos
  become a shared package or sidecar.
- **Ship Kaminos-backed shell:** a project packages a Kaminos chamber, renderer,
  or cartridge host as part of its product surface.
- **Archive prototype:** the route remains design evidence and future reference.

Graduation accounting records source-owned dependencies, Kaminos-owned
affordances, shared runtime candidates, project repo patches, retained cartridge
features, and witness artifacts.

## Preview Benches

A **Preview Bench** is an inspection posture for chamber evidence before that
evidence becomes an inhabited chamber, promoted asset, accepted route result, or
cartridge feature.

Preview Benches display source-owned payloads through Kaminos-owned host
surfaces. They keep source payload identity, source authority, freshness,
fixture/fallback/stale state, badge rendering, and browser witness capture
visible in one surface.

The first concrete instance is the LERMS/Underhill-style terrain preview bench.
A future material bench, splat correction bench, motion bench, or
object-interaction bench can reuse the same source-honest intake shape while
keeping its own domain vocabulary.

## Motion And Body Custody

Kaminos can preview generated motion, store promoted motion takes, display
cliplet sheets or filmstrips, and place motion payloads into chambers and
cartridges. This is host custody: spatial placement, badges, capture, camera,
and witness shape.

The source adapter owns payload semantics and freshness. The worldbody and
motion layers own motion grammar, event assignment, embodied dynamics, and
behavior truth until explicit source evidence promotes a result. A LERMS actor
timeline, Mushfinger cliplet sample, or future body-motion payload can be
hosted in a chamber while preserving its source vocabulary.

For Forge stations, the same boundary holds as positive ownership. A station
can advertise Smoke Offers, show status, receive operator observations, and
connect the workbench to embodied presence.

## Smoke Offers

A **Smoke Offer** is the handoff from a producing lane, station, cartridge, or
future agent orb to a smokeable inspection surface.

The offer says: this producer has something the operator can inspect; here is
the target surface, source route, source ref, payload schema, authority,
freshness, downgrades, and target bench. It is a station-facing affordance that
connects production work to operator smoke.

The current preferred contract shape is:

```text
kaminos.forge-host.smoke-offer.v0
```

Preview Benches can mount those offers through a route/state/witness layer, for
example:

```text
kaminos/preview-bench/smoke-offer-file
kaminos.preview-bench.smoke-offer-state.v0
preview-bench-smoke-offer-contract
```

The contract preserves `live`, `fixture`, `fallback`, and `stale` as separate
authority and freshness axes. A live packet can become stale. A fixture can be
fresh as fixture. A fallback may be recent while carrying downgraded authority.
Kaminos shows a simple badge to the operator while keeping the underlying
authority, freshness, downgrades, and display state machine-readable.

## Operator Smoke Capture

The return path matters as much as the opening path. When an operator smokes a
generated piece or cartridge scene, Kaminos can produce operator smoke capture
evidence: screenshots, filmstrips, camera identity, active route, source
authority, payload schema, timestamp, and an optional operator note.

That lets a producer receive:

- the exact route the operator inspected,
- the payload and source refs on screen,
- the camera or preset used,
- the capture artifacts,
- the authority/freshness/downgrade state visible at capture time,
- and the operator's note anchored to that evidence.

This is the beginning of a shared visual conversation: the operator and the
producer can talk about the same visible object.

For example, a motion take can export a filmstrip with prompt, route, model,
source frame range, camera preset, reference skeleton mode, authority,
freshness, downgrade state, and the operator's note. The producer receives a
small visual object both sides can discuss.

## Source-Honesty Rules

Kaminos makes evidence more visible while preserving its source strength.

Required public-facing rules:

- Preserve source schemas and routes.
- Show source authority, freshness, fixture/fallback/stale downgrades, and
  failure phase.
- Record effective route/config identity alongside requested inputs.
- Treat screenshots and filmstrips as evidence with source and camera context.
- Keep debug and acceptance surfaces labeled by role.
- Surface fallback data with fallback authority.
- Treat host display as display custody and source packets as domain-truth
  custody.

The same rule already applies to splat assets. Kaminos can store correction
sidecars and render editor previews while the source splat file remains the
complete source asset. See [Splat Assets](splat-assets.md) for that contract.

## Relationship To The Inhabited Agent Forge

The Inhabited Agent Forge is an internal and future-facing extension of this
architecture.

The long-term direction is that source lanes and diauloi can become embodied
stations in Kaminos. An operator could walk up to a station, see what it is
working on, open its Smoke Offers, inspect the visible object on a bench, capture
evidence, and route the observation back. The kiln and cartridge layers give
that future agent forge an honest spatial substrate.

Publicly, Kaminos leads with the Spatial Asset Kiln: spatial workbench,
source-honest evidence, generated asset conditioning, route witnesses, chambers,
benches, cartridges, terrariums, and inspectable material process. The
agent-forge layer can emerge as the demo becomes coherent enough to be seen
through those surfaces.

# Separate wet spots and short liquid bridges

The `wetting: 'regions'` option is a provisional extension of the neighbor/arc/spiral crystal model. `wetting: 'single'` preserves the preceding one-spot-per-side comparison; old saved states retain that behavior. New native Crystal growth objects select regions. The inspector's model, layout, lean and bridge-reach choices apply on Restart layout, which keeps a checkpoint. Supply, evaporation, mobility, growth and birth controls affect subsequent evolution immediately. They do not erase prior geometry or salt.

A crystal side owns a sparse list of wet regions, each with water, dissolved salt, dry residue and a birth clock. An incoming contact wets its actual location. Disconnected arrivals do not mix. A region's footprint is a union of rectangular lobes; overlapping/touching regions combine supplies and retain that union rather than filling its bounding rectangle. Drying can disconnect lobes again, partitioning the formerly mixed contents by their shares. This is a reduced film law: lobe shape and volume shares approximate spreading, and normalized face coordinates move with the crystal side. It does not resolve a fine fluid interface or within-film concentration gradients.

Existing contacts connect exposed sides around a grain and a newborn to its parent. The new geometric search also finds short opposed faces on neighboring grains, including newborns. Projected overlap selects a representative passage. Its opening, length and width are recomputed from the current grains; a solid crossing its center line blocks it. Narrower passages have lower viscous conductance. The search is spatially indexed. The explicit bridge reach (default .08 local length units) is a provisional wetting-distance assumption, not a population cap or calibrated physical constant. A single representative passage does not resolve all parallel channels across a broad overlap or general corner menisci.

A gap retains the water and salt that fill it. An advancing meniscus draws from a wet contact until that volume is filled. Then liquid can flow between the two local spots, flushing the retained bridge salt as a mixed volume. A shrinking gap returns excess liquid; a closed gap traps its contents and stops flow. Evaporation can break a filled connection. Closed-pore growth, dissolution of solid grains and cracking are outside this model. Unplaced dry salt remains accounted residue rather than being silently converted to decorative grains.

Growth and birth consume the same spot's supersaturated salt. Half a growth step precedes birth and half follows, avoiding unconditional first access for either reaction. Birth uses the outside boundary of the retained wet footprint, not internal rectangle seams. Its concentration-dependent rate and initial cube size remain phenomenological. Larger existing grains and more new grains are competing outcomes to explore with the two rates and supply history, not presets claimed to reproduce particular salt micrographs.

Individual grains stay cuboids. A wet side's allocated solid volume moves that entire planar side; nearby solids can halt it. Thus an attached child can constrain parent growth. This retained approximation is the current comparison, not proof that local relief within a side is unnecessary. New geometry influences transport on the next physical step. Opaque instancing and retained film geometry remain the presentation path; this adds no raymarch or scattering integration.

Useful observations: opposite-end arrivals retain different concentrations; touching spots mix and can separate on drying; a newly narrowed or blocked gap changes later supply; a filled bridge feeds an initially dry neighbor; increasing growth competes with birth from the same finite salt. Conservation and exact save/restart are necessary checks. They do not establish an attractive floor, terraces, a validated geological model, or a crowded-scene frame budget.
